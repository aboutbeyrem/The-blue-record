    Liner notes

    Data: NASA Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS).

    Country borders, sea names and cities: Natural Earth (public domain). Borders are shown as published there and are not a statement by NASA or by us. Mars facts: NASA Mars Fact Sheet. Made at the NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, Tunis. Not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA. Values are read from the colors of NASA’s published maps using NASA’s own color tables, so they are as fine as those tables.

    How to listen

    Pick a country

    1. Spin the globe and click a country, or search for it. “Use my location” picks yours.
    2. Choose what to hear: temperature, rain, height of the land or greenness. NASA’s newest data is fetched live.
    3. One scan line sweeps the country from west to east. Higher and louder means more. Silence means no reading. Move the line north or south, and each pass moves time forward.
    4. Mars bling. When the data matches Mars, such as a place as cold as Mars’s average day, you hear a chime.

    The whole-planet record

    1. The record is the Earth. The centre is the pole, the edge is the equator. Side A is the northern half, Side B the southern.
    2. The tonearm is latitude. One spin carries the needle once around the planet along that latitude.
    3. Each spin moves time forward, so you hear the same latitude change through the year.
    4. The sound is the data.
